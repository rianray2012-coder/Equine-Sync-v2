# EquineSync — Post-Launch Observation Log
_Started Feb 20 2026 · the listening artifact · open this every week_

> This is not a product backlog. It is a behavioural truth log.
>
> Each entry should describe what someone actually did inside the barn —
> not what we think it means. The interpretation comes later, after the
> patterns accumulate.
>
> **Tone rule.** Observational, not evaluative. The platform is not
> defending itself. We are watching.

---

## How to write a good entry

A good entry is **a specific behaviour, witnessed or self-reported, on a specific day**, written without adjective.

Good:

- _"Sophia took a photo of the dewormer schedule on the whiteboard at 5:14am instead of opening Today."_
- _"Owner Charlotte read Tuesday's digest at 06:42 and forwarded one paragraph to her insurance agent."_
- _"Trainer Marcus scheduled three lessons back-to-back and the soft conflict warning fired on the second one; he scheduled it anyway."_
- _"Eleanor reopened the Approve / Decline composer twice before sending."_
- _"Groom skipped the bulk-mode toggle and tapped each turnout row individually for 11 of 14 horses."_

Avoid:

- _"Users disliked the dashboard."_
- _"The conflict warning failed to prevent…"_
- _"Workflow was problematic when…"_
- _"Feature wasn't used."_

If the observation contains a feeling word ("frustrated", "confused", "loved"), rewrite it as the behaviour that produced the feeling word.

## Standing reflection — review this every Friday

After accumulating a week of entries, ask:

1. Which section has the most entries? That is where the platform is most being lived.
2. Which section is empty? That is either uncovered ground or behaviour we are not yet seeing.
3. What did someone do this week that surprised us — in either direction?
4. Where is the tone of the platform reading right? Where is it reading wrong?
5. What did we almost change this week, but waited?

Do not act on any of these answers in isolation. Let them accumulate for at least three weeks before reprioritizing the backlog.
